# ROCKNIXDS 1.6.4 for the RG DS Plus
**A hotfix for the audio crackle in some DS games.** Since 1.6.3, people have reported crackling sound in some DS
games on the RG DS Plus. It doesn't happen in every game: on the handheld we tested it on, it showed up in demanding
3D scenes (a Mario Kart DS race was the worst) and hardly or not at all in lighter ones (Pokémon Black 2 and
HeartGold walking around). This release changes how SuperDrastic paces DraStic so its sound shouldn't run out any
more. **This fix is not fully tested yet: we need to hear from you** (see the end of these notes).

## Install or upgrade

From 1.6 or 1.5: **Updates & downloads > ROCKNIXDS** in the menu. From anything else, ssh into the handheld as `root`
(password `rocknix`) and run:

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JorreFog/ROCKNIXDS/main/install.sh | sh
```

New SD card: the 1.6 image on the [v1.6-plus release](https://github.com/JorreFog/ROCKNIXDS/releases/tag/v1.6-plus),
then update from the menu.

## 🔊 What was wrong

DraStic only keeps about 35 ms of sound made ahead of what the speaker plays. If SuperDrastic asks for sound that
DraStic hasn't made yet, DraStic hands back what it has and **fills the rest with silence**. A few milliseconds of
silence in the middle of the music is a click, and many clicks in a row sound like crackle.

Two things in 1.6.2 and 1.6.3 could make DraStic that late:

- **The frame queue's hold.** With the *balanced* and *battery* power profiles, when the screens' frame queue is full,
  SuperDrastic holds DraStic for up to 20 ms instead of dropping a frame (that keeps the picture smooth). In a heavy
  scene, a 20 ms hold plus a slow frame was more than DraStic's 35 ms of sound could cover.
- **The CPU clock stepping down too far.** The power profiles let the CPU go down to 1104 MHz when a game looks
  light. In a race, it stepped down, the game fell behind for a moment (a burst of clicks), the clock went back up,
  and a minute or two later it tried again.

The *performance* profile doesn't hold DraStic, which may be why some people never heard it.

## 🔧 What changed

SuperDrastic 0.5.0-beta.1-rocknixds.10 (everything else is 1.6.3-plus):

- **The hold lets DraStic go in time.** SuperDrastic now watches how much sound DraStic has ready, and stops holding
  it while that sound still covers DraStic's next frame (how long its recent frames took, plus 4 ms). When that
  happens the queue drops a frame instead, the way it did before the hold existed. Light games keep the hold as it
  was.
- **A clock that makes the game fall behind isn't tried again.** If the sound runs out or a frame is dropped within
  5 seconds of the CPU stepping down, it goes straight back up and stays off that lower clock for the rest of the
  game. You hear at most one short burst instead of one every minute or two. Games that really are light still run
  at the lower clocks and save battery.
- **The game log says when the sound ran out.** Every 10 seconds `dsflip.log` has a line like
  `[audio] DraStic's sound: at least 900 frames ahead, ran out 0 times (0 frames of zeros)`. Until now no log showed
  these gaps: the audio counters (underruns, xruns) said 0 while the sound clicked.

No setting needs changing. It costs no speed: the extra work is reading two numbers from DraStic's memory once per
5.8 ms.

## 🧪 How we tested it, and how we got there

All on one RG DS Plus (the battery profile, the ds-grid-2x shader, the Gengis Engine at 2x), starting games from
the menu the normal way, from savestates, one minute at a time:

1. **Ruled out the usual suspects.** PipeWire and the speaker amp reported no errors during play; the speaker's
   clock was exactly 48 kHz; pressing the volume keys during play didn't cause it; 4 minutes of HeartGold had no
   gaps at all. Recording the speaker's output found no clipping.
2. **Found where the silence comes from.** Reading DraStic's audio code (it's a closed binary) showed that it fills
   missing sound with zeros and keeps only ~35 ms ahead. We added a counter for exactly that.
3. **Measured per game** (gaps a minute, the 1.6.3 library):

   | Game (scene) | Gaps a minute |
   |---|---|
   | Mario Kart DS (a race) | 18 – 33 |
   | Metroid Prime Hunters (title) | 0 – 5 |
   | Pokémon Platinum (from a save) | 0 – 4 |
   | Dragon Quest Monsters Joker (title) | 0 – 1 |
   | Pokémon Black 2, HeartGold (walking) | 0 |

4. **Found the cause by changing one thing at a time** (Mario Kart race): with the hold off, 0 – 6 gaps a minute
   (but 64 – 168 dropped frames, a visibly choppy picture); with the CPU locked at its top speed, still 26. So the
   hold was the main cause, not a slow CPU. The leftovers came right after the clock stepped down.
5. **Tested the fix** in the same games and scenes (the 1.6.3 numbers are from step 3):

   | Game (scene) | Gaps a minute, 1.6.3 → 1.6.4 | Dropped frames a minute, 1.6.3 → 1.6.4 |
   |---|---|---|
   | Mario Kart DS (a race) | 18 – 33 → 1 – 5 | 0 → 2 |
   | Metroid Prime Hunters (title) | 0 – 5 → 5 | 0 → 2 |
   | Pokémon Platinum (from a save) | 0 – 4 → 0 | 0 → 10 |
   | Dragon Quest Monsters Joker (title) | 0 – 1 → 3 | 0 → 0 |
   | Pokémon Black 2 / HeartGold (walking) | 0 → 0 | 0 – 2 → 0 |

   These are single one-minute runs, and the same game varies a lot from one run to the next (Mario Kart on 1.6.3:
   32, 18 and 18 in three races). Mario Kart, the one game where the crackle was clearly there, went from a click
   every 2–3 seconds to a few a minute; the remaining ones came while the CPU was still speeding up. In the other
   games the numbers moved within the noise; Metroid Prime Hunters' title screen still had a few gaps, at the lowest
   clock (1104 MHz), which this fix doesn't touch. Platinum dropped more frames in its run, around one moment where the
   clock stepped down and straight back up; we don't know yet whether that happens in real play.
6. **Tried things that didn't make it in:** making SuperDrastic wait for DraStic's sound made it worse (its own
   audio started running dry); letting DraStic keep more sound ahead (52 and 70 ms) didn't reliably help and adds
   delay between picture and sound (still there to try: `DSFLIP_AUDIO_AHEAD=<ms>`); a fixed 25 ms limit on the hold
   cut the gaps further but dropped 8 – 16 frames a minute in games that never crackled.

## 🙏 Please tell us how it sounds

This fix is **not 100% tested**. It was measured on one handheld, in six games, a few minutes each, with one set of
settings. We don't know yet:

- whether the games you heard crackling are fixed (other games may be late for other reasons);
- how it behaves in the *balanced* profile, at 3x, with other shaders, or after the handheld has been on for hours
  or slept (a different crackle, where every game crackles until a reboot, was seen once on a test build and is
  **not** addressed here);
- whether the picture drops a few more frames in some games than before (the hold now gives way to the sound).

If you still hear crackle, please open an issue with the game, the scene, your power profile and shader, and
`/storage/.config/drastic/dsflip/dsflip.log` from right after it happened (or turn on sharing performance logs in
the DS settings). The `DraStic's sound: ... ran out` lines will tell us whether it's this problem or another one.

Tested on an RG DS Plus: the library through the menu's normal game start, six games, before and after.
